Project

NTU–A*STAR joint project on spatial intelligence for manufacturing robots.

NTU–A*STAR Joint Project · WP 2.2

Spatially-Grounded Perception and Planning for Embodied AI Agents

Build a reusable, planner-ready spatial layer that bridges perception and planning for manufacturing robots.

Project objectives

  • Connect perception to planning. Build a reusable spatial layer that organizes the objects, places and relationships a manufacturing robot needs to plan its actions.
  • Support long-horizon, safe mobile manipulation. Combine navigation and pick-and-place in dynamic, semi-structured shop-floor environments.
  • Validate reusability. Evaluate two manufacturing demos and transfer across at least two robot embodiments or layouts with minimal reconfiguration.

Application 1: planetary gearbox assembly

A mobile dual-arm robot explores the workcell, builds an object graph and selects a task-dependent chain of objects. It collects gear components from separate tables, assembles the gearbox, retrieves a wall-mounted driver, fastens the cover and returns the tool.

Of 16 nominal tests, 0/7 grasps, 2/6 insertions and 0/3 fastening stages pass; six additional timestep/control checks complete the 22-case audit. Application 2: pharmaceutical sample handling

A Ranger Mini mobile base with a lifting carriage and two PiPER arms carries out a complete 15-step training work order across five laboratory stations: materials storage, liquid handling, mixing, centrifugation and finished-sample storage. It collects and identifies sample S01, adds wash solution, mixes and centrifuges the sample, retains the pellet, resuspends it and prepares two labeled aliquots, S01-A and S01-B, before inspection, storage and cleanup.

The replay connects recorded semantic observations to an authored Mission Graph. The incremental Object Graph joins objects, stations and the room; mission dependencies and equipment gates control the work order; and the Interaction Graph shows current actions, grasps and support relationships. Target-tube preparation runs alongside centrifugation. Unloading becomes available only after preparation is complete, the rotor has stopped and the lid is unlocked.

This is a kinematic training replay with an authored Mission Graph and symbolic liquid, barcode and equipment acknowledgements. Object boxes are simulation annotations from visible rendered instance masks. Tubes have modeled support fixtures; the robot and objects pass sampled geometric intersection checks at 120 Hz. Demonstration timings are not production parameters. Physical process validation and cross-embodiment evaluation remain project objectives.
